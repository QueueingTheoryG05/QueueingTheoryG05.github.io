Practical problem

What Is Delayed Matching in Ride-Hailing?

Instantaneous matching

Match as soon as a request arrives

Passengers are assigned immediately, but the available driver may be far away.

Delayed matching

Wait briefly, then match in a thicker pool

More passenger-driver combinations can reduce pickup distance and pickup waiting time.

The platform controls when to release buffered participants into the assignment problem.

Paper Fig. 1.

Core trade-off

Why Can Delayed Matching Be Better Than Instantaneous Matching?

\[\Delta t\uparrow\quad\Rightarrow\quad W_{\mathrm{matching}}\uparrow\]
\[\Delta t\uparrow\quad\Rightarrow\quad W_{\mathrm{pickup}}\downarrow\]
\[W_{\mathrm{total}}=W_{\mathrm{matching}}+W_{\mathrm{pickup}}\]

The opposing slopes create a U-shaped total-wait curve and an optimal matching interval.

Key differences

How Is This Queue Different from the Classical Queues We Studied?

It speaks the language of queueing theory, but it is not a standard \(M/M/1\) or \(M/M/c\) queue.
Difference 1

Both sides can wait

Passengers and drivers may both accumulate in buffers.

Difference 2

Matching precedes service

A passenger-driver pair must be formed before pickup begins.

Difference 3

Batch service epochs

Matching happens every \(\Delta t\), not continuously.

Difference 4

Pickup wait matters

Waiting cost includes both matching wait and pickup wait.

Difference 5

Carryover changes the future

Unmatched participants move to the next interval.

Difference 6

Spatial dependence

Pickup time depends on distance and grid location.

Problem formulation

What Exactly Is the Queueing Problem Being Solved?

Control variable

Hold or Match

Continue buffering or release participants for matching.

Objective

Minimize total waiting cost

Balance matching wait against pickup wait.

Core variables

\(\lambda_P,\lambda_D,N_P,N_D,\Delta t\)

Arrival rates, queue populations, and matching interval define the state.

Patience behavior

Balking and reneging

Participants may leave if expected waiting exceeds \(t_{\max}\).

The queueing problem is to choose the matching interval so that total passenger waiting is minimized over repeated decision epochs.

Analytical assumptions

What Assumptions Are Used in the Deque Approximation?

01

Poisson arrivals

Passenger and idle-driver arrivals use stationary rates \(\lambda_P\) and \(\lambda_D\).

02

Uniform locations

Locations are uniformly distributed over the service area.

03

Periodic matching

Matching occurs at \(\Delta t,2\Delta t,3\Delta t,\ldots\).

04

Carryover

Unmatched participants remain for the next window.

05

Pickup estimate

Pickup wait is Manhattan distance divided by speed \(v\).

06

Patience threshold

Waiting beyond \(t_{\max}\) can cause balking or reneging.

Stationary arrivals allow a fixed \(\Delta t\) benchmark; non-stationary arrivals require adaptive control.

State evolution

How Do Hold and Match Decisions Update the Queues?

\[\text{New queue population}=\text{Previous queue population}+\text{New arrivals}-\text{Successful matches}.\]
Hold: \(i\in S_0\)\[\begin{aligned}N_P(i,t)&=N_P(i,t-1)+n_P(i,t-1:t),\\N_D(i,t)&=N_D(i,t-1)+n_D(i,t-1:t).\end{aligned}\]

No match is released in this grid yet, so both buffers simply accumulate the new arrivals.

Match: \(i\in S_1\)\[\begin{aligned}N_P(i,t)&=N_P(i,t-1)+n_P(i,t-1:t)-m_i(t),\\N_D(i,t)&=N_D(i,t-1)+n_D(i,t-1:t)-m_i(t).\end{aligned}\]

A successful matching step removes \(m_i(t)\) paired passengers and drivers from the buffers.

Stationary-arrival benchmark

What Is the Fixed Matching-Interval Strategy?

Stationary case

Search for one best fixed \(\Delta t\)

If arrivals are stationary, delayed matching becomes equivalent to choosing the optimal constant matching interval.

Queueing interpretation

The best delay depends on the supply-demand gap

When \(\lambda_P\approx\lambda_D\), a longer delay is more valuable because a thicker pool can improve pair quality.

From stationary to real operations

Why Does a Fixed Matching Interval Break Down Under Non-Stationary Arrivals?

Why fixed \(\Delta t\) works only in theory

Stationarity keeps the trade-off stable

If the rates do not change, one constant interval can represent the environment.

What changes in practice

Time and space shift the queue regime

Arrival rates vary by region and period, so the best interval changes.

Operational consequence

The interval must become adaptive

One fixed \(\Delta t\) cannot fit balanced and imbalanced conditions simultaneously.

Queueing simulator

How Does the Simulator Execute the Queueing Logic?

01

Buffer requests and idle drivers

New arrivals and unmatched participants remain in the queueing buffers for the next decision epoch.

02

Choose Hold or Match

Hold preserves the current batch; Match releases the available participants to the assignment stage.

03

Solve the matching assignment

A global bipartite assignment pairs passengers and drivers to reduce pickup time; unmatched participants carry over.

Position in the literature

How Does the Paper Bridge Queueing Models and Adaptive Control?

Analytical queueing and MDP models

Interpretable structure

They explain how arrival rates, waiting buffers, matching epochs, and service capacity shape system behavior.

Their weakness is that they usually rely on stationary or simplified assumptions.

Research gap Real ride-hailing systems are spatial, non-stationary, high-dimensional, and affected by earlier matching decisions.
Data-driven reinforcement learning

Adaptive policy search

It can learn a matching policy from simulated trajectories when the state is large and transition dynamics are difficult to model exactly.

The queueing simulator supplies the structured environment in which that policy is trained.

Paper Fig. 5.

Global matching stage

Timing First, Minimum-Cost Assignment Second

Linear sum assignment problem \[ \begin{aligned} \min_{x_{ij}}\quad &\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}T_{ij}x_{ij}\\ \text{s.t.}\quad &\sum_{j=1}^{n}x_{ij}=1,\qquad \sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{ij}=1,\\ &x_{ij}\in\{0,1\}. \end{aligned} \]
Edge cost

\(T_{ij}\) is the pickup time from driver \(i\) to passenger \(j\), estimated from Manhattan distance and speed \(v\).

Unequal queue lengths

The cost matrix is padded to \(n=\max(N_D,N_P)\) using a large value \(M\).

Carryover

Unmatched passengers and drivers return to the buffer and influence the next state.

Two-stage logic

RL decides when and where; optimization decides who matches with whom.

Learning mechanisms

Policy Gradient Methods Learn the Hold/Match Policy Directly

Policy Gradient

Optimize a parameterized policy

Adjust \(\pi_\theta(a\mid s)\) in the direction of higher expected return.

REINFORCE

Monte Carlo return

Uses sampled future return \(G(t)\) to update the policy, with high variance.

Actor-Critic

Policy plus value estimate

The Actor selects actions; the Critic estimates long-run value and enables bootstrapping.

ACER

Actor-Critic with experience replay

Reuses experience to improve sample efficiency and stabilize training.

\[G(t)\leftarrow R(t)+\gamma V_w(O(t+1))\]

Implementation adaptations

Three Adaptations Enable Spatial Delayed Matching

01

Action-scheme mapping

A one-dimensional action ID is mapped to a multi-binary Hold/Match matrix with up to \(2^{|S|}\) schemes.

02

Experience replay

Previously sampled transitions are reused, increasing sample efficiency and reducing step-wise correlation.

03

Partial-episode bootstrapping

Episode time limits are not treated as true terminal states, preventing biased value updates.

Why ACER?Rare, correlated, high-dimensional experiences should not be discarded after one update.

Reward-design challenge

A Naive Reward Arrives Only When Matching Occurs

Problem

Holding appears artificially attractive

When the action is Hold, the immediate reward can remain zero while matching produces a negative waiting cost.

Consequence

Weak credit assignment

The agent struggles to connect a long sequence of Hold actions to a distant eventual cost.

Remedy

Step-wise decomposition

Spread matching and pickup waiting penalties across the passenger's queueing lifetime.

Paper Fig. 6.

Queue-aware objective

The Reward Separates Matching-Wait and Pickup-Wait Costs

Matching-wait reward\[R_m(t)=-\sum_{i\in S}\left[N_P(i,t-1)+\tau_m\bigl(n_P(i,t-1:t)\bigr)\right]\]
Pickup-wait reward\[R_p(t)=R_p(S_0,t)+R_p(S_1,t)\]
Weighted total reward\[R_w(t)=c_mR_m(t)+c_pR_p(t)\]

Operational meaning

\(R_m\) penalizes every extra step spent waiting for a match. \(R_p\) credits or penalizes the change in expected pickup cost caused by holding or matching.

Experiment weights\(c_m=4,\qquad c_p=1\)Matching uncertainty is treated as more costly than post-match pickup waiting.

Empirical queue behavior

The U-Shape Appears Only in a Moderate Supply-Demand Regime

Moderate occupancy

\(70\%\)

Total wait cost is U-shaped: pickup savings initially exceed the added matching delay.

High or low occupancy

\(\ge 80\%\) or \(\le 50\%\)

Total wait generally rises with the interval; instantaneous matching is preferred.

The slope of pickup wait, not matching wait, determines whether the U-shape emerges.

Episodic policy interpretation

Balanced Active Queues Trigger Delayed Matching

Instantaneous matching

Selected when \(N_D\gg N_P\) or \(N_P\gg N_D\), especially when arrival rates are low or stable.

Controlled delayed matching

Selected when \(N_D\approx N_P\) and \(\lambda_P,\lambda_D\) are sufficiently high to create valuable alternatives.

Spatial heterogeneity

Different grids follow different time-varying probabilities because queue populations and arrival rates evolve differently.

The learned network can be interpreted as a practical lookup rule based on queue balance and activity level.

Paper Fig. 13.

Model limitations

The Simulator Simplifies Patience, Mobility, and Service

Passenger patience

A fixed patience threshold is used rather than heterogeneous behavior.

Driver movement

Idle drivers remain in their current grids instead of repositioning.

Driver participation

Drivers are assumed to remain active and do not leave the system.

Single rides

The model excludes ride-pooling and the associated matching-queue structure.

Offline training

The policy learns in a simulator rather than through live platform interaction.

Architecture reporting

Exact network layer sizes and activations are not specified in the paper.

Conclusion

Delay Helps Only When Queueing Gains Exceed Added Wait

Queueing insight

Balanced and active supply-demand states create the strongest opportunity for delayed matching.

Control insight

RL adapts the matching epoch across space and time while assignment optimization handles pair selection.

Evidence

ACER with \(3\times2\) grids reduces mean total wait by \(20.41\%\) versus instantaneous matching.

\[ \boxed{\text{Best policy}=\text{queue-aware timing}+\text{minimum-cost assignment}} \]
